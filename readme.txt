Owners-only records for Florida condos and HOAs: every record in its place, dated, with meeting notices on the front page.

== Description ==

Florida condo associations with 25 or more units, and HOAs with 100 or more parcels, keep their official records on a website with a page only owners can open. CondoClock turns your WordPress site into that place: owners' records behind a login, and meeting notices where every owner can find them.

= What you get =

* Every record the statute lists, as a checklist: see at a glance what's up and what's missing.
* Upload a file and it's on the owners' page, with the date it went up.
* A Notices page for upcoming meetings, linked from your front page.
* Private files: only logged-in owners and staff can open them.
* Add owners and give them their logins.

Setup takes a few minutes. Pick condominium or HOA, enter the number of units or parcels, and CondoClock creates the Owners' Records page and the Notices page, adds them to your menu and shows you the list of records to post. Drag the documents you already have onto their row and owners can read them that day.

Every category shows the section of Chapter 718 or 720 that lists it, and the date the rule table was last checked. Board members can mark a category as not applicable, with a reason owners can read.

Files go into a private folder that visitors can't open by guessing the link. CondoClock tests this itself and tells you, with the exact fix to send your host, if your server shows the files anyway.

Owners' pages come in English, Spanish and Haitian Creole.

= CondoClock Pro =

CondoClock Pro puts a clock on every record and every meeting, so the board sees what's due before it's late. It reminds you by email, lets owners request a login and approves it in one click, keeps a dated history you can export, and warns you when a file holds emails, phone numbers or other private details before it goes up.

USD 149 a year for one association, or USD 449 a year for up to 10 associations, for web designers and managers.

CondoClock isn't legal advice. Your association's attorney decides what your documents and deadlines are.

== Frequently Asked Questions ==

= Which records does a Florida condo association post? =

Section 718.111(12)(g)2 lists 18 kinds, from the declaration, bylaws and rules to budgets, financial reports, contracts, bids, meeting notices and agendas, and building safety reports. CondoClock shows each one as a row with its letter from the statute. For HOAs, section 720.303(4)(b)1 lists 13.

= How many days before a meeting does the notice go up? =

For a condo, section 718.112(2)(c)1 asks for board meeting notices at least 48 hours before, and 14 days before a meeting on special assessments or unit-use rules. Notices of owners' meetings go up 14 days before, and the documents to be voted on 7 days before. CondoClock lists upcoming meetings on the Notices page, soonest first. CondoClock Pro counts down each deadline for you.

= Can a management company run the site? =

Yes. The statute allows the records to be on the association's own website or on a third-party website or app that the association has the right to use. A manager or web designer can run CondoClock for the association, and CondoClock Pro's 10-site plan covers up to 10 associations.

= Is my data sent anywhere? =

No. The free plugin makes no outside requests. Records, files and owners' details stay in your WordPress site. The only exception is the file protection test, where your site asks itself for a test file to check that strangers can't open it.

= Does CondoClock decide whether our records satisfy the statute? =

No. CondoClock organises and dates your records. Whether a record meets the statute is your attorney's call.

= My host uses nginx. Are the files safe? =

Nginx ignores the protection file CondoClock puts in the private folder. The Status screen tests this. If files are open, it shows the one rule your host needs to add, and a warning stays on every admin screen until it's fixed.

= What happens to my records if I remove the plugin? =

Nothing, unless you tick "Delete all data when uninstalled" in Settings. It's off by default.
